# Lucky draw reveal: the prompt behind the animation

`website/public/lucky-draw.html` - the reveal played on the draw page - was generated by Claude from a style prompt
plus our own brief, then refined over many rounds. The brief below is kept in sync with what the animation does now,
so it can regenerate something equivalent, or be adapted for another challenge (change the prize amounts, the jokes
and the voice-over).

## Regenerating it

The style is **"Flat-Vector Science"** from Futurepedia's [AI Style Atlas](https://www.futurepedia.io/resources/ai-style-atlas/view).
Its prompt (the style guide, Claude's build instructions, a helper library, a sound kit and a reference animation's
source) is Futurepedia's (all rights reserved), so only our parts are kept here:

1. On the Style Atlas, copy the "Flat-Vector Science" prompt.
2. Replace its "MY CONTENT" section with [ours](#my-content).
3. In its "WHAT TO BUILD" section, replace the control bar and `scoreSound` bullets with [ours](#what-to-build-changes).
4. Use the H helper library and SOUND kit from `lucky-draw.html` rather than the copied ones: the sound kit there
   has fixes for seeking (long sounds join in progress instead of failing) and a `clip()` primitive for the
   voice-over.

The voice-over script, its generation and its timings are in [voiceover.md](voiceover.md).

## MY CONTENT

```text
A fitness challenge's lucky draw results: reveal the top 20 prize winners. Everyone watches it themselves on the challenge's /draw page, which draws the winners (from a public drand random beacon) and then plays this animation in an iframe.
- Branding: the challenge's name, uppercased (pink pill in the opening and closing beats, small corner header "<NAME> · LUCKY DRAW" in between; the pill text shrinks for long names). It comes from the URL hash ("app", the website's branding) and defaults to "Summer Bodies Challenge"; the page title is "<name> - Lucky Draw". Title: "LUCKY DRAW", "RESULTS ARE IN!".
- Input: the winners and the entry count come from the URL hash, #d=<URI-encoded JSON {"names": [1st place, ..., 20th place], "entries": n, "app": "challenge name"}>. Without it (a preview), use the placeholder "Name Surname" for every winner and 50 entries. Read it once into NAMES (20 names, 1st place first) and ENTRIES.
- Facts: ENTRIES entries, 20 winners. Prizes: places 20 to 4 win $20 each, 3rd $60, 2nd $110, 1st $210 ($720 in total).
- Theme: keep the Flat-Vector Science look (gradients, crescent shadows, glows, big-eyed round characters, grain finish) but NOT space. Use a sunrise running scene: dawn sky, rising sun, clouds, rolling hills, a running track, round runners with sweatbands and sneakers, medals on ribbons (gold, silver, bronze). A giant orange sun sweeping across is the scene-change wipe.
- Story: hook (sunrise, runner jogging, title) -> 50 balls on a running track (always 50 balls, but the counter counts up to ENTRIES) plus a gold medal showing 20 -> 20 balls lift off the track as gold medals ("20 of ENTRIES drawn") -> sun wipe -> "17 × $20": a raffle drum (spinning cage of balls on a stand, with a chute) on the left pulls places 20 to 4 out one at a time - every 0.75 s from 9.3 s, a ball shoots out of the chute along a dotted arc and lands as a name card ($20, no place numbers - they all win the same) in a two-column list of 9 + 8 on the right, in draw order (20th place first); the drum turns steadily with a quick extra spin before each ball and empties a little each time, and the list stays up until the beat ends at 23.2 s -> "Top 3" podium: 3rd ($60) and 2nd ($110) with their names and runners wearing medals, but 1st ($210) stays a mystery - a glowing, pulsing "?" ball in place of the runner and a yellow "???" name tag -> dive into the gold medal: "1st PRIZE", "$210", and only now the 1st place name -> pack of 20 runners: "Congratulations!" "to all 20 winners". The 1st place name must appear only once, on the gold medal beat.
- Long names: real names can be long ("Parker van der Westhuizen", "Casey Montgomery-Fitzgerald"). On the drum list's name cards a name must never run into the prize: shrink it to fit (down to 26px), else wrap it onto two lines split at the most balanced space or hyphen (up to 26px, down to 18px), and only cut it with an ellipsis if even that can't fit. The podium and 1st prize name tags may just grow wider.
- Humour, matching the tone of the challenge's announcements (fake trademarks like "FitCoin®", mock-legalese, "bribe the organiser"): small cream captions on clear space, never over names or prizes, each on screen long enough to read (about 2.5 s or more):
  * 2.3-4.8 s, bottom: "Every FitCoin® is a raffle ticket. Every ticket is a dream."
  * 4.9-10.0 s, bottom, on a dark pill and drawn above the sun wipe so it stays up while the first names come out of the drum (until 10 s): "Picked by a beacon run by the League of Entropy. Yes, that's a real thing."
  * 17.5 s to the end of the drum beat, under the drum: "Still not you? Blame drand." / "Not the organiser. Definitely not the organiser."
  * 35.6 s to the end, under "to all 20 winners": "Everyone else: you still won fitness (not redeemable for cash). See you next year!"
- Pacing: this is a results reveal, so reveal steadily (a name every 0.75 s from the drum) and keep each full results screen visible a moment before moving on (about 2 s for the 1st prize and the final line). Total about 37 s. Do not use the word "ranks" in headings.
- Voice-over: ten recorded clips next to the HTML file - voiceover/line1.mp3 ... line9.mp3, plus line6b.mp3 ("And... first...", drawn out so it leads straight into the $210 hit) - starting at 0.0, 2.82, 5.85, 9.0, 17.4, 23.4, 28.07 (line6b), 30.8, 33.03 and 34.0 s (script and timings: voiceover.md). Load and decode them as the page opens; once loaded, scoreSound schedules each with k.clip(t, buffer) and the player plays in voiceover mode ({ voiceover: true }), so beds drop out and minor effects duck. If they fail to load, play without them. If they finish loading mid-play, restart the sound so the voice joins in.
- Sound: footsteps, stopwatch ticks for counters, starter-pistol crack at the title, a drum rattle as it spins up, a pop as each ball leaves the chute and a clink as it lands (rising as the list fills), medal clinks, marimba plucks, soft pad underneath; biggest hit when "$210" lands.
- Player: as in WHAT TO BUILD, plus a "Full screen" button (whole page, works inside the /draw page's iframe; label "Exit full screen" while on; hidden where the browser can't do it, e.g. iPhone Safari). Loop is on by default, but off when real winners are passed in, so a reveal stops on the final frame.
- Delivery: after the storyboard is approved, also render an MP4 (1280x720, 30 fps, with the sound muxed in) using node and puppeteer-core with the local Chrome. For an MP4 with the real winners, load the file with the same #d=... hash (the repo's functions/examples/render-reveal.ts does this from a draw link).
```

## WHAT TO BUILD changes

```text
- A 1280x720 canvas, scaled to fit the window with letterboxing, and a small control bar: play/pause, a scrub slider, the current time, a sound on/off toggle, and a loop toggle.
- A `scoreSound(k)` function that schedules the sound effects with the SOUND kit below (see SOUND). Play it in sync from the player: on play call `stopSound = SOUND.live(scoreSound, currentT, { voiceover: false })` (in a try/catch, so a sound failure never breaks the controls), and call `stopSound()` on pause and when the sound toggle is turned off. On seek, call `stopSound()` on every slider step but start the sound again only once the slider settles (about 200 ms after the last step) - each SOUND.live call creates a new AudioContext, and browsers refuse more after a handful.
```
